# Deck Grill question bank

Pick, don't recite. Skip anything the source material already answers; confirm it instead. Each branch lists the core question, follow-ups for vague answers, and what "good enough" looks like. Thai phrasings are given for the core questions.

## 1. Objective
- **Core:** After this presentation, what should the audience decide, do, or be able to do? / หลังฟังจบ อยากให้ผู้ฟัง "ตัดสินใจ" หรือ "ทำ" อะไร
- If "inform / update / share knowledge": And once they know it, what changes? What goes wrong if they don't know it?
- If several objectives: Which one matters if only one happens?
- Is there a hidden objective (build credibility, get budget later, get invited back)?
- **Good enough:** one observable outcome, e.g. "Ops managers approve a 3-month pilot of the digital tower dashboard at Don Mueang."

## 2. Audience
- **Core:** Who exactly is in the room (roles, seniority, number)? Who decides? / ใครฟังบ้าง ใครเป็นคนตัดสินใจ
- How much do they already know about the topic? Jargon OK or not?
- What do they care about most: money, risk, time, safety, reputation, their team's workload?
- What will they object to or be sceptical about? What has failed before?
- What's their relationship with the presenter (boss, client, peers, students)?
- Mixed audience? Whose needs win when they conflict?
- **Good enough:** a named primary persona with their top concern and top objection.

## 3. Core message
- **Core:** If they remember only one sentence, what is it? / ถ้าผู้ฟังจำได้แค่ประโยคเดียว อยากให้จำประโยคไหน
- Could a sceptic disagree with it? (If nobody could, it's a platitude — sharpen it.)
- What are the 2-4 supporting points that make it believable?
- **Good enough:** one arguable sentence + 2-4 supporting points.

## 4. Call to action
- What exactly should happen next, by whom, by when?
- What's the smallest yes you'd accept today?
- **Good enough:** a concrete next step that can go on the last slide.

## 5. Context
- Presented live, recorded, or sent as a file to read? (Reading decks need more text; live decks need less.)
- How long is the slot, including Q&A?
- Where: meeting room, big hall, video call, phone screen?
- Language and tone: formal / friendly / playful / technical?
- Who presents, and how comfortable are they with the material?
- **Good enough:** mode + minutes + tone + language.

## 6. Content and evidence
- What must be in the deck (data, case studies, product features, regulations, demos)?
- What must NOT be in it (confidential numbers, competitor names, sensitive topics)?
- What's the strongest proof you have? Numbers, before/after, a customer story, a demo?
- Any existing material to reuse: draft, video, report, old deck, screenshots?
- Stories or analogies the audience will relate to?
- **Good enough:** the evidence for each supporting point is known or flagged as missing.

## 7. Format and constraints
- Style: hand-drawn step-by-step (sketch-deck HTML), corporate PowerPoint, or both?
- Slide count or time limit? Brand colours, logo, template?
- Will it be shared afterwards (then slides must stand alone)?
- Deadline?
- **Good enough:** format + count range + any brand rules.

## 8. Success criteria
- How will you know it worked a week later?
- What question would you love them to ask at the end? Which one do you dread?
- **Good enough:** a measurable or observable signal.

## Pressure questions (use when answers stay vague)
- "Imagine it's over and it went badly. What happened?"
- "What would the most sceptical person in the room say at slide 3?"
- "If you had to cut half the slides, which ones survive?"
- "Why should they care about this today, not next quarter?"
- "What do you know that they don't?"
